# Ebook Drafting — Style Guide and Format

## Output Format (every draft)

```
# [Title]
## [Subtitle]

**[Edition]** — Scholarly / General Public / Children's

Elie Schulman

---

## Table of Contents

1. [Chapter title]
2. [Chapter title]
...

---

### 1. [Chapter title]

[Content]

### 2. [Chapter title]

[Content]

...

---

*[Source citations]*
```

## Three Registers

**Scholarly:** Academic register. Hebrew/Aramaic citations with translations. Philosophical analysis. Close readings. References to primary texts, Sefaria URLs. For the reader who arrives through the text itself.

**General Public:** Story-driven, accessible, bold provocations. Concrete examples without requiring Hebrew. Opens with a scene or a question. Short paragraphs. The reader who arrives through a question, not a source text.

**Children:** Simple sentences, concrete imagery, relatable analogies. Same eight-chapter structure. "About eight hundred years ago..." register. No dumbing down of the ideas — just simpler language.

## Key Principles

- Same TOC structure for all three audiences (same chapters, different titles)
- Each draft must be a complete, standalone ebook
- TOC at the beginning — reader can scan it and know the main points
- Grounded in actual source texts (Maimonides, Ibn Tibbon, Talmud)
- The word is not the meaning. The meaning is not the word.
- Source citations at the end with URLs

## File Naming

`/home/user/workspace/drafts/[topic-slug]-[audience].md`

Examples:
- `emes-truth-scholarly.md`
- `emes-truth-general.md`
- `emes-truth-children.md`
