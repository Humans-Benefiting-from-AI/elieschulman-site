### 2. Who Is emet?

Maimonides begins his big book with a foundation.

The first words are: "The foundation of all foundations and the pillar of wisdom is to know that there is a Primary Being."

He doesn't try to prove it. He doesn't argue. He just says: this is where we start. There is a First Being. Everything else follows from that.

Like how a math book starts with rules, not with proofs. You have to start somewhere.

---

Then he builds, step by step:

**Step 1:** The Primary Being brought everything into existence. Everything — the sky, the earth, everything in between — came from the emet of His being.

**Step 2:** If you imagine He doesn't exist, nothing else could exist either. Everything depends on Him.

**Step 3:** If you imagine nothing else exists, He would still be here. He doesn't need anything. Everything needs Him; He needs nothing. So His emet doesn't look like anyone else's emet.

Notice something: the word emet has already shown up twice. And both times, it's about existence — about what it means to really be. Not about sentences being accurate.

---

Then Maimonides gets to the main sentence.

He quotes a verse from Jeremiah: "And God, your Lord, is emet."

And he explains it: "He alone is emet, and no other has emet like His emet."

This is the sentence the whole book turns on. Let's read it slowly.

**"He alone is emet."** Not "He alone is true." Not "He alone is truthful." He alone is emet.

God doesn't have emet the way you have a toy — as something you could hold or lose. God is emet. Emes is what God is, not what God has.

**"No other has emet like His emet."** Other things exist. Maimonides doesn't say they don't. But their emet is not like His emet. The word is the same. The reality is different.

Think of it like this: a real diamond and a glass diamond are both called "diamonds." They share the name. But one is real and one is an imitation. The name crosses between them. The reality doesn't.

God's emet is real existence — existence that doesn't depend on anything. A created thing's emet is borrowed existence — real, but depending on God. Same word. Different thing entirely.

---

Then Maimonides adds his own explanation. He quotes another verse: "There is nothing else aside from Him."

And he explains: "That is to say: there is no existent emet aside from Him like Him."

The word for "existent" is מָצוּי. Maimonides is saying: aside from God, there is no existent whose existence is emet. He is putting emet and existence together. Not "there is no other true thing." There is no existent whose way of existing is emet — genuine, not borrowed, not depending on anything else.

Everything else exists, but its existence is borrowed. Like a lamp that shines because it's plugged in. The lamp is really shining. But the electricity comes from somewhere else. The lamp's light is real, but it's not its own.

Only God's existence is emet. Everything else is a lamp.

---

So here's what emet means:

Emet is the quality of genuine existence — existence that doesn't depend on anything else. Only God has it. Everything else exists, but in a borrowed way.

This is not about sentences being true. It's about what it means to really exist.

---

And here's the big implication — the one Maimonides doesn't stop to explain, but leaves for the reader:

If emet is genuine existence, and only God has it, then no created being has emet. Not because we don't exist — we do. But our existence is not emet. It's real but borrowed. Like the lamp.

The difference between God and us is not that God exists more and we exist less. It's that God's existence is emet and ours is something else entirely. Real, but not emet.

And if our existence is not emet, then our knowledge is not emet either. We can know things. We can be right about things. But we can't have emet — because emet is what God is, not what we have.

That's what the next chapter explains. But the seed is already planted right here. God alone is emet. Everything else exists, but not with emet.
