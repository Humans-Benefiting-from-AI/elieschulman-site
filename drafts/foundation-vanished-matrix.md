# If the Foundation Vanished — Source-to-Argument Matrix

## Yesodei HaTorah 1:5-6

### Root passage

אֱלוֹהַּ זֶה אֶחָד הוּא וְאֵינוֹ שְׁנַיִם וְלֹא יֶתֶר עַל שְׁנַיִם. אֶלָּא אֶחָד. שֶׁאֵין כְּיִחוּדוֹ אֶחָד מִן הָאֲחָדִים הַנִּמְצָאִים בָּעוֹלָם

*This God is one. He is not two or more, but one. A unity unlike any unity found in the world.*

— Maimonides, Mishneh Torah, Foundations of the Torah 1:6

The word "one" is misleading. The word "unity" is misleading. Maimonides says so himself — the oneness of God is not like any oneness we know. The word crosses, but the meaning doesn't. The same problem as emet.

---

## How the root passage generates the argument

Three movements:

1. **"One" — but not like any one we know.** Maimonides immediately qualifies: not one like a category that contains many, not one like a body that can be divided. Every "one" we encounter in the world is either a collection or a divisible thing. God's oneness is neither.

2. **"Unity" — but the word misleads.** יִחוּד (unification) implies bringing things together. But God was never multiple. There is nothing to unify. The word for unity describes a process that never happened — because God was always one.

3. **Why plurality is impossible for God.** If there were many gods, they would have bodies (because only bodies can be distinguished from each other). But God has no body. Therefore God cannot be more than one. The unity is not a theological preference. It is a logical necessity that follows from incorporeality.

## The arc

- **Chapter 1:** The word "one" — Maimonides says God is one, then immediately says: not like any one you know. The word is the same. The reality is different. Same problem as emet. The word crosses; the meaning doesn't.
- **Chapter 2:** What God's oneness is NOT — not a category (one species containing many individuals), not a body (one thing divisible into parts). Every "one" in our experience is one of these two. God's oneness is neither.
- **Chapter 3:** The word "unity" — יִחוּד implies bringing together what was separate. But God was never separate. The word describes a process that never occurred. The wrong word for something that has always been the case.
- **Chapter 4:** The logical ground — why one follows from incorporeal. If there were many, they would need bodies to distinguish them. God has no body. Therefore God cannot be more than one. The unity is not a preference or a theological claim. It is what follows necessarily from having no body.

The root passage returns: שֶׁאֵין כְּיִחוּדוֹ אֶחָד מִן הָאֲחָדִים הַנִּמְצָאִים בָּעוֹלָם. A unity unlike any unity in the world.
