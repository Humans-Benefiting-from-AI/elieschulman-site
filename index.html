About My Work

About

Elie Schulman

I am an educator, group facilitator, writer, and founder of Cohort Learning Labs. My work examines how people think and learn together—especially when familiar roles, inherited language, or rapid technological change make adaptation difficult.

I am particularly interested in problems that return after intelligent people have already tried to solve them. Repetition is not merely proof that the last solution failed. It may reveal something about how a group distributes authority, protects identity, manages uncertainty, or prevents itself from learning what it does not yet know.

Through Cohort Learning Labs, I work with businesses, professional teams, organizations, families, and group leaders. My writing explores related questions through Jewish texts, translation, intellectual history, law, and the changing meaning of intelligence.

Based in Beit Shemesh, Israel, I work with people and organizations in Israel and the United States.

Artificial Intelligence

AI is a human adaptation problem

Artificial intelligence is arriving as a tsunami of change. Its speed is unprecedented; human adaptation is not. Groups are being asked to revise how they work, learn, decide, and exercise authority faster than they know how to absorb.

The hardest questions are therefore not only technological. They concern what people fear losing, which roles and identities the old system protected, who is permitted to know, and how a group responds when familiar expertise no longer provides stable ground.

Emes: Translation Not Truth

By Elie Schulman

This book begins with a translation problem — and follows it into questions about existence, knowledge, disagreement, and what any human being can claim to know.

Writing

Lines of inquiry

My writing returns to a connected set of questions: how translation carries assumptions, how inherited categories guide judgment, and how people learn to see the structures shaping an argument, a relationship, or a group.

Jewish texts and translation

What becomes visible when familiar translations are tested against Hebrew vocabulary, context, and the intellectual worlds in which a text was written.

Language and judgment

How words, categories, and unstated assumptions organize professional and intellectual decisions without determining them.

Learning and group life

How people think together, how groups respond to uncertainty, and how technology changes the conditions under which shared understanding develops.
