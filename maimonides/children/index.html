Words and Names

What happens when a word pretends to be a thing? When a name makes you think you know more than you do?

Book · Young Reader's Guide

Beyond Either/Or: A Young Reader's Guide to Careful Words and Clear Thinking

Some arguments feel impossible because the words were never properly defined. This book shows younger readers how to ask what a word means before choosing a side — and how sometimes the best answer is: I don't yet accept the question.

Book Series

Words and Names as Labels

The first book in a series for younger readers. What happens when we label things — and forget that we made the labels? Intelligence, smart, stupid: words that sort people without telling us what they actually mean.

Book

Naming Sights of the Eye

What we see and what we call it are not the same thing. A book about the gap between looking and naming — for readers learning that their eyes and their words don't always agree.

Book

Naming Sights of the Mind

The eyes have it easy. The mind is harder — it sees things that aren't there, misses things that are, and names everything before it understands anything. A book about thinking and the words that shape it.

Decisions and Choices

How do you decide when you don't have all the words? When the choices you're given might not be the only choices?

Book · Outline

A Children's Book on Decisions

What happens when a child is asked to choose between two things — and discovers that maybe those aren't the only two things? A book about the moment before the question, when the words haven't yet closed the doors.

Seeing and Knowing

What shows up before you decide what it is?

Essay · For Younger Readers

Eyes Open, Eyes Closed

Close your eyes: a sound, a feeling, a thought. Open them: colors, shapes, objects. The pictures changed. The showing-up didn't stop. That's what appearing means — and it's the first thing to notice before any word takes over.

Exercise

Exploring Thoughts Phenomenologically

An exercise for younger readers: notice a thought. Don't explain it. Don't fix it. Just watch it arrive, stay, and leave. What changes when you stop trying to understand and start trying to notice?

The Word "Intelligence"

What does it mean when someone says you're smart? And what does it mean when someone says a machine is?

Book

The Word Intelligence

"Intelligent" used to describe what someone did. Then it became a thing inside their head. Then it became something you could measure. Then a machine started doing the things that counted. What happened to the word — and what happened to us?
