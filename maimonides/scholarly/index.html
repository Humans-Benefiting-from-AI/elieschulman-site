Maimonides / Scholarly Edition

The source spine in chronological order

Primary texts, original-language citations, philosophical analysis, and close reading. The order is shared with the General Public and Children's editions; the apparatus is not.

I. Early works

1 · Treatise on Logic, Ch. 10

Kinds of Predication in the Treatise on Logic

Four classes of predication, definition versus description, and the conflation that distorts.

2 · Treatise on Logic, Ch. 14

Inner and Outer Speech: The Non-Theological Foundation

The passage from thought to utterance and the limits present “in any language whatsoever.”

3 · Commentary on the Mishnah / Shemoneh Perakim

The Soul in Shemoneh Perakim

The soul, its faculties, ethical formation, prophecy, and the confusions produced by shared names.

II. Mishneh Torah — Hilkhot Yesodei HaTorah

4 · 1:1–1:4 · Approved matrix

Emet: What the Word Points To (Not “Truth”)

Seventeen chapters rooted in הוּא לְבַדּוֹ הָאֱמֶת. Chapters 1–2 are approved. Recovered Chapter 1 and Chapter 2 components remain separately preserved pending a lineage-explicit canonical assembly.

Active matrix · 1:2 · Approved

The Thought Experiment of 1:2: Phenomenological Dependence

Four chapters distinguishing structural dependence from temporal causation. This is identified as the author's interpretation, not Maimonides' explicit terminology.

5 · 1:5–1:6 · Approved revised matrix

“One” as Equivocal: The Problem of Divine Unity

Four chapters: neither category nor body, the problem in the word “unity,” and incorporeality as the logical ground of oneness.

6 · 1:7–1:12

Divine Unity and Accommodated Scriptural Language

Unity without body, scriptural accommodation, divine emotions, and language beyond place and time.

7 · 2:1–2:2

Love, Awe, and the Contemplation of Creation

Commanded dispositions, contemplation, and wonder as the beginning.

8 · 2:3

Three Created Categories: Ontology of Change and Invariance

Changing matter and form, invariant celestial matter and form, immaterial form, and the architecture of change.

9 · 2:4–2:9

Angels, Ranks of Form, and Created Knowledge of God

The hierarchy of form, created knowledge, and the limits of each rank.

10 · 2:10

The Unity of Knower, Known, and Knowledge in God

Why divine knowledge is not external and why attribution produces a category error.

11 · 4:7

The Indescribable: Consciousness and the Invisible Seer

Berakhot 10a, the eye of the heart, reflexive knowing, and the boundary between seer and seen.

12 · 4:8

False Dichotomies: The Danger of Names

Equivocal, derivative, and amphibolous terms; nefesh and ruach; definition before division.

III. Guide, letter, and translator's apparatus

13 · Guide Introduction; I:1, I:50–57, I:64–65

In Any Language Whatsoever: The Limits of Utterance

Predication, inner and outer speech, equivocal terms, belief and conception, attributes, and the material production of speech.

14 · Guide I:46, I:64–65

The Problem of Soul-to-Soul Communication

The sensory paradox, the material bridge, and the distinction between epistemic limitation and metaphysical impossibility.

15 · Letter to Samuel Ibn Tibbon

The Translation Rule: Meaning, Not Word Order

Understand the sentence, render its sense, and recognize the gap the rule does not close.

16 · Perush ha-Millot ha-Zarot

The Glossary: Why Translation Requires Apparatus

The technical-vocabulary problem and the conceptual apparatus behind every transferred word. Bibliographic verification remains pending.

Approved reference manuscript

Approved · Complete · 8 chapters

Ibn Tibbon's Preface: A First-Person Philosophy of Translation

The complete Scholarly draft is preserved with a visible table of contents. It is the style reference for the scholarly register and remains distinct from the Emes project.
