Rambam's Writings — Chronological

Organized by Maimonides' own writings in chronological order, with source texts, translations, and commentary attached to each. For the reader who arrives through the text itself.

← Maimonides Library

1. Treatise on Logic (מקאלה פי צנאעת אלמנטק)

Rambam's earliest work — an introduction to logic written in his youth, in Judeo-Arabic. The Efros 1938 trilingual edition (Hebrew, Arabic, English) is scanned and indexed in the seforim research toolkit (195 pages, with a lookup tool and text index).

Elie's commentary: No standalone essay yet. The Treatise on Logic is referenced in the broader language-and-translation work as an early example of Rambam's attention to the relationship between words and the realities they point to.

2. Commentary on the Mishnah (פירוש המשניות)

Rambam's commentary on the Mishnah, written in Judeo-Arabic between ages 23 and 30. Includes the introductions to several tractates, most notably:

  • Shemoneh Perakim (Eight Chapters) — introduction to tractate Avot. An ethical-philosophical treatise on the nature of the soul, virtue, and prophecy.
  • Introduction to Helek (Sanhedrin, Chapter 10) — the famous essay on the afterlife, resurrection, and the world to come, including the Thirteen Principles of Faith.

Source texts: Referenced in the knowledge wiki. Specific scanned editions not yet located in the seforim toolkit.

Elie's commentary: The Shemoneh Perakim is referenced in the broader work on "self" and "soul" — the question of what the names for the soul refer to and how easily they confuse. The Thirteen Principles are pressure-tested in the Emes book's pressure_test_13_principles.md.

3. Mishneh Torah (משנה תורה)

Rambam's comprehensive code of Jewish law, completed c. 1180. Organized into 14 books. Elie's primary source text is the Warsaw 1881 edition, Vol. 1 (Sefer HaMadda and Sefer Ahavah), 778 pages, Hebrew — scanned, indexed, and searchable via the seforim CLI.

Sefer HaMadda (Book of Knowledge)

Hilkhot Yesodei HaTorah (Foundations of the Torah)

The most extensively developed area of the Maimonides project. 20 scanned pages (pp. 23–42) with SHA-256 verification, individual page PDFs, a source and claims lock, comparative translations, and a 12-sequence chronological series map.

1:1–4 · Scan pp. 23–24

Underived Divine Existence

Every other existent depends upon His truth/reality. The opening of the entire system. Elie's writing: Emes: Translation Not Truth (17 chapters, published) — the word אֱמֶת does not cross into English as "truth" without distortion. Also: No Truth Like His Truth outline, Truth Languaging materials. Diplomatic transcription Pass 01 complete.

1:5–6 · Scan p. 24

First Existent as God; Knowledge and Unity as Commandments

Bridge within Volume 1. Continues 1:1–4; anticipates 1:7. Elie's writing: Truth/emes family. Chapter and page verified; transcription pending.

1:7–12 · Scan pp. 24–25

Divine Unity, Incorporeality, and Accommodated Scriptural Language

Language about the incorporeal. Links unity to epistemic language. Elie's writing: Truth Languaging, Ineffable Variants, Words That Point Beyond Themselves (Foundations 1:9), When Scripture Speaks of God's Emotions (Foundations 1:12), Maimonides on God, Language, Attributes (research dossier). 1:7 source commentary quarantined pending verification.

2:1–2 · Scan p. 26

Love and Awe Through Contemplation of Creation

Introduces three created orders. Elie's writing: Love and Awe Are Commanded (Foundations 2:1), The Path Begins With Wonder (Foundations 2:2). Opening verified; transcription pending.

2:3 · Scan p. 26

Major book candidate: change, becoming, and created existence. Pairs directly with 4:5; contrasts change and invariance. Elie's writing: Everything Changes: What Maimonides Meant (published), Maimonides' Transient Existence Research (dossier), Three Kinds of Created Being (Foundations 2:3). 2:10 · Scan p. 28

God's Knowledge Is Not External to Him: Knower, Known, and Knowledge Are One

Later deepening of the truth/existence question. Overlaps 1:1–4 but should not duplicate it. Elie's writing: Emes (governing source), Knowledge Is Not Something God Has (Foundations 2:10), The Shape That Knows (essay). Diplomatic transcription Pass 01 complete.

Mada Series — Ibn Tibbon Reframings

A sequence of reframings of Yesodei HaTorah that treats translation as "carrying-across" — making the reader feel that each rendering is a decision. Series Introduction: The Carrying-Across, anchored in Ibn Tibbon. Volume 1 (1:1) curated; Volumes 2–5 (1:2–1:5) in progress.

4. Guide for the Perplexed (Dalālat al-Ḥāʾirīn / Moreh Nevuchim)

Three editions in the seforim toolkit, each scanned and indexed:

  • Judeo-Arabic original (Dalālat al-Ḥāʾirīn) — 847 pages
  • Hebrew translation by Samuel Ibn Tibbon — 622 pages, Lvov 1866, with commentaries by Shem Tov, Efodi, Crescas, and Abravanel
  • English translation by M. Friedländer — 486 pages, 1904

Term concordance spreadsheet (guide_term_index.xlsx). Extracted PDFs for negative attributes (I:58–60) and senses as source of knowledge (I:46–47), in both Hebrew and English.

Elie's writing on the Guide:

Essay

The Phenomenology of Translation: Ibn Tibon's Philosophical Hebrew

What Ibn Tibbon understood about translation — that carrying-across is not matching but deciding. The translator who made the reader feel each rendering as a choice.

Essay

The Phenomenology of Translation: Ibn Tibon's Introduction to the Guide

Ibn Tibbon's introduction as a philosophy of translation in its own right — not a preface but an argument about what words can and cannot carry.

Essay

Thoughts on Maimonides' Philosophy of Language

What Rambam's treatment of equivocal names reveals about the philosophy of language — and about shared vocabulary masquerading as shared meaning.

Essay

Maimonides on God, Language, and Attributes

The language-and-attributes problem: how words that describe God can simultaneously reveal and conceal. Every attribute is both necessary and dangerous.

5. Letter to Samuel Ibn Tibbon

Rambam's correspondence with his Hebrew translator, in which he advises Ibn Tibbon on how to render philosophical terms. Ibn Tibbon's introduction to the Guide (pp. 2–4 scanned) is the anchor for the Mada Series' The Carrying-Across.
