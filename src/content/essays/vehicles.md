  <h2 id="groups">Psychoanalytic Groups</h2>
  <p>Eighteen years of training in group psychoanalytic process — with Louis Ormont, PhD; Michael Brook, PhD; and Sean Grover, MSW. T-group methodology in the Carl Rogers tradition. The group as the primary learning technology: not the content, not the platform, but the quality of attention between people.</p>
  <p>The group reveals what no individual can see alone — how authority distributes without being spoken, how language operates between people in real time, how a group prevents itself from learning what it does not yet know. The source sheets on Pirkei Avot and Mishneh Torah emerged from this practice. The facilitation guides and the cohort-based learning model are its direct application.</p>

  <h2 id="sitting">Silent Sitting</h2>
  <p>The practice of pausing before what appears. Not meditation, not mindfulness, not a promise of calm or spiritual outcome. A real interval in which there is nothing to accomplish.</p>
  <p>The practice arises from the Talmudic instruction to linger for an hour before prayer. The hour is not a performance test. It is the space where what appears before explanation — sounds, sensations, thoughts, impulses, moods, even blankness — can be noticed before it is named. This is where the questions about "self," "soul," and "names" were not theorized about but encountered directly.</p>
  <p>The practice has its own site at <a class="work-link" href="https://www.yishhe.org">yishhe.org</a>.</p>
