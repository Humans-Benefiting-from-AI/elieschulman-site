Psychoanalytic Groups

Eighteen years of training in group psychoanalytic process — with Louis Ormont, PhD; Michael Brook, PhD; and Sean Grover, MSW. T-group methodology in the Carl Rogers tradition. The group as the primary learning technology: not the content, not the platform, but the quality of attention between people.

The group reveals what no individual can see alone — how authority distributes without being spoken, how language operates between people in real time, how a group prevents itself from learning what it does not yet know. The source sheets on Pirkei Avot and Mishneh Torah emerged from this practice. The facilitation guides and the cohort-based learning model are its direct application.

Silent Sitting

The practice of pausing before what appears. Not meditation, not mindfulness, not a promise of calm or spiritual outcome. A real interval in which there is nothing to accomplish.

The practice arises from the Talmudic instruction to linger for an hour before prayer. The hour is not a performance test. It is the space where what appears before explanation — sounds, sensations, thoughts, impulses, moods, even blankness — can be noticed before it is named. This is where the questions about "self," "soul," and "names" were not theorized about but encountered directly.

The practice has its own site at yishhe.org.
